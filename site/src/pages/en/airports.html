Online Monitoring for Airports

One substation fault can affect the whole operation, and maintenance windows are short.

An airport runs terminals, airfield lighting, baggage systems and air navigation equipment from its own substations and a private medium-voltage network. A fault in one substation can affect the whole operation, and maintenance windows are short. Continuous monitoring allows the condition of transformers, cables and supply quality to be followed without taking equipment out of service.

01The power chain

A small utility inside a fence.

An airport operator is, in effect, a distribution company with one very demanding customer. The chain looks like this.

  1. 01

    Grid supply

    The connection to the public network. Everything downstream depends on it.

  2. 02

    HV supply cables

    The routes into the site. Joints and terminations are the points to watch.

  3. 03

    Intake substation transformers

    The step down to the airport’s own network. An internal fault here means loss of the main supply.

  4. 04

    Private MV network

    The ring or radial network that links the substations across the site.

  5. 05

    Terminal and airfield distribution transformers

    Many units, spread over a large area, some in places that are hard to reach during operations.

  6. 06

    Supplies to sensitive systems

    Electronic loads that react to dips and harmonics long before the lights do.

The operation runs around the clock. The equipment has to be understood without stopping it.

03What is different

What makes an airport different.

AP·01

Short windows.

Outages for testing have to fit around the operation. Measurement that needs no outage fits a site that rarely offers one.

AP·02

Wide area.

Substations and transformers are spread across terminals, aprons and airfield. A continuous reading from each saves a visit to each.

AP·03

Mixed loads.

Lighting, drives, conveyors and electronics share one network. A disturbance caused by one can be felt by another. A record of what the voltage did settles the question of where it came from.

04Questions

Questions this sector asks

Q1We have standby supplies for essential systems. Why monitor the main chain?

Standby supplies cover an interruption. Monitoring aims to see a fault developing in the main chain before it becomes one.

Q2Which asset comes first?

Usually the intake substation transformers. They carry the whole site, and a replacement takes a long time.

Q3We have many small transformers across the site. Can they all be monitored?

Not with a full system on each. The practical approach is one compact oil-condition sensor per unit, used to rank the population and direct inspections.

Q4Sensitive equipment trips and nobody knows why. Can monitoring help?

A fixed power quality analyser records dips, harmonics and other events with time stamp and waveform. Matching those records with equipment logs is how such trips are usually explained.

Q5Can the results be brought into one view for the maintenance team?

Yes, that is the purpose of asset performance management software. See Asset Management.
