Cable & MV Monitoring

The asset is a route, not a single item. Watch the known weak points. Locate faults fast.

The weak points are spread along kilometres of circuit. Monitoring therefore has two tasks: watching known weak points on high-voltage cables, and locating faults quickly on medium-voltage feeders.

Two on one page

This page covers two separate solutions: one for high-voltage cable circuits, one for medium-voltage overhead lines. They share a page because both deal with a route. They do not share a product.

01Two tasks

On a cable, watch the points. On a line, find the section.

A

Task A · HV cable circuit

Watch the weak points.

A high-voltage cable is manufactured and tested in a factory. Its joints and terminations are assembled on site, by hand, in a trench or on a structure. That is where most of the risk sits. They are also spread along the route, often in places that are hard to reach.

Three quantities say a lot about them. Phase current shows the load each cable carries. Screen current shows whether the metallic screen and its bonding are behaving as designed. Temperature shows whether a joint or termination is running hotter than its neighbours.

B

Task B · MV overhead feeder

Find the fault.

On a medium-voltage overhead feeder the question is rarely the condition of insulation. It is location. A feeder may run for many kilometres with branches. When protection trips, someone has to find where. Some faults do not trip protection at all: a broken conductor or a high-impedance fault may draw too little current to be seen from the substation.

Sensors mounted on the line measure current and voltage at points along it. Software compares what the sensors saw and narrows the fault down to a section.

On a feeder, the task is locating a fault on a long line, not assessing insulation.

02What it detects

Three conditions on the cable. Three events on the line.

AHV cable

Screen current
Abnormal screen (sheath) currents at joints and terminations.
Overheating
Temperature at joints and terminations.
Load imbalance
Unequal phase currents along the circuit.

BMV overhead feeder

Fault passage
Which sensors saw the fault current, so the faulted section is identified.
Broken conductor
An open conductor, with the section identified.
High-impedance fault
A fault that draws too little current for conventional detection.

03Reading it

Compare with the neighbours, with the load, and with the next sensor.

AHV cable

Screen current against design
The screen bonding of a cable circuit is designed so that screen currents stay within an expected pattern. A change in that pattern points to the bonding system, the links or the outer sheath, usually at or near a joint.
Temperature against neighbours
Joints on the same circuit carry the same load. One that runs warmer than the others, at the same current, is the one to look at.
Everything against load
Temperature and screen current rise and fall with load. Reading them together with phase current separates a normal busy day from an abnormal condition.

BMV overhead feeder

Between which sensors
A fault is located between the last sensor that saw it and the first that did not. More sensors mean shorter sections.
Voltage and current together
A broken conductor may show as a loss of current or an abnormal voltage downstream, not as a fault current. Measuring both is what makes it visible.
Patterns over time
Repeated short events on the same section can point to a developing problem before a permanent fault.

Terms used on this page

Joint
The connection between two lengths of cable.
Termination
The end of a cable where it connects to other equipment.
Screen (sheath)
The metallic layer around the cable insulation, bonded to earth.
Screen current
Current flowing in that metallic layer.
Passive sensor
A sensor that needs no power supply at the measuring point.
High-impedance fault
A fault with too little current to operate conventional protection.
Fault passage
An indication that fault current flowed past a given point.

07After an alarm

On the cable, inspect a point. On the line, dispatch to a section.

AHV cable

  1. 01 · Alarm

    A joint or termination stands out.

    Abnormal temperature or screen current.

  2. 02 · Compare

    Against load and the other points.

    Check against load and against the other monitored points on the circuit.

  3. 03 · Inspect

    Plan a site inspection.

    Of the joint bay, link boxes and bonding at that location.

  4. 04 · Decide

    Load, repair or watch.

    Adjust loading, schedule a repair or continue to watch.

BMV overhead feeder

  1. 01 · Event

    The software reports a fault.

    And the section it lies in.

  2. 02 · Dispatch

    The crew goes to that section.

    Instead of patrolling the whole feeder.

  3. 03 · Restore

    Isolate the section.

    Switching isolates the section; supply to the rest is restored.

  4. 04 · Review

    Look for repeats.

    Recurring events on the same section are reviewed as a possible developing problem.

General sequences. Switching and dispatch procedures belong to the network operator.

09FAQ

Questions we are asked

Q1Does Greenlight measure partial discharge in the cable?

No. Per manufacturer data it measures phase current, screen current and temperature at joints and terminations.

Q2Is Greenlight for medium-voltage cable?

No. It is a system for high-voltage cable circuits.

Q3Are MS5200 and Megger Grid Analytics the same thing?

No. MS5200 is the sensor on the line. Megger Grid Analytics is the software that manages the sensors and analyses their data.

Q4Where does the analytics software run?

Per manufacturer data, on a Megger-hosted cloud server or on the customer’s own server.

Q5We have underground MV cable. What applies?

MS5200 is an overhead-line sensor. Tell us about the network and we will check the suitable route with the manufacturer.
