Online Monitoring for Data Centers

Redundancy hides a slow insulation fault. It does not remove it.

A data center is designed around redundancy, but every redundant path still starts at the same medium-voltage intake and passes through transformers, switchgear and cables. A slow insulation fault in that chain is not removed by redundancy; it is only hidden by it. Monitoring shows the condition of each link while the load stays online.

01The power chain

From the grid connection to the data hall.

Follow the line. Each station is a link the load depends on, and each one ages out of sight.

  1. 01

    Grid connection

    Where the site meets the network. Supply quality is set here before anything on site can influence it.

  2. 02

    HV grid connection cable

    Often a single route. Its joints and terminations are the points to watch.

  3. 03

    Main and step-down transformers

    Under continuous high load, with little opportunity for an outage to test them.

  4. 04

    MV switchgear and main busbars

    Where the redundant paths divide. Also where voltage quality for the whole site can be measured.

  5. 05

    Standby generators

    Idle most of the time, expected to work at once. Insulation ages whether the machine runs or not.

  6. 06

    Distribution to the halls

    Many smaller transformers and boards, fed from the same upstream chain.

  7. 07

    The whole chain, across sites

    One operator, several sites, no single view of condition unless someone builds it.

Redundancy protects the load, not the equipment.

03The blind spot

Why redundancy is not the answer to this.

DC·01

A redundant path is still equipment.

The second transformer is the same age, the same design and under the same conditions as the first. Common conditions produce common ageing.

DC·02

A failover is a stress, not a rest.

When one path is lost, the other takes the full load at once. That is the worst moment to discover its condition.

DC·03

Operation gives no signal.

A transformer with a developing fault delivers the same voltage as a healthy one. The building management system shows green until it does not.

General engineering reasoning. No design or tier claims are implied.

04Questions

Questions this sector asks

Q1We have N+1 or 2N. Why monitor the equipment as well?

Redundancy covers the loss of one path. Monitoring tells you the condition of the path you would be left with. The two answer different questions.

Q2Our transformers cannot be taken out for testing. What then?

That is the case online monitoring is for. Partial discharge and dissolved gas are measured while the transformer carries load.

Q3Our generators hardly run. Why would their insulation deteriorate?

Winding insulation ages with time, temperature cycles, moisture and contamination, not only with running hours. On a machine that rarely runs, a problem has few chances to show itself before the day it is needed.

Q4Our load is mostly power electronics. Does that matter upstream?

Non-linear loads draw distorted current and raise harmonic levels on the supply. A Class A analyser at the intake and main busbars records harmonics and dips, and helps show whether a disturbance came from the grid or from the site.

Q5We operate several sites. How do we compare them?

With a common asset record and common assessment rules. That is the role of asset performance management software. See Asset Management.

Q6What about racks, PDU, UPS and ATS/STS?

Those are supply items, not monitoring. See Supply & Project Solutions.
