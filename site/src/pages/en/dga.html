Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA) Monitoring

Online gas measurement gives an early warning between laboratory oil samples.

When a fault develops inside an oil-filled transformer, the oil and paper insulation break down and release gases that dissolve in the oil. Hydrogen and acetylene are the two gases that carry most of the early-warning information.

Read this first

This page is about a two-gas online monitor. It warns that something is developing. It does not replace laboratory DGA, and it does not identify the type of fault.

01The phenomenon

The oil keeps a record of what happens inside the tank.

A transformer’s insulation is oil and paper. Under normal operation both age slowly. Under abnormal stress, whether electrical or thermal, they decompose faster and release gases. Those gases dissolve in the oil and circulate with it.

Which gases form depends on how much energy the fault releases. Low-energy processes produce mainly hydrogen. Very high temperatures and arcing produce acetylene. Other gases sit in between, and their proportions are what a laboratory uses to tell fault types apart.

The oil therefore carries information that cannot be seen from outside the tank. A laboratory sample reads it in detail, at intervals. An online monitor reads part of it, all the time.

A laboratory sample tells you a great deal, occasionally. A monitor tells you less, continuously. You need both.

02What it detects

Two gases and the water content. Three readings, three meanings.

Gas 01 · general signal

H2

Hydrogen

Produced by most developing faults, including partial discharge and overheating, so it is a general early indicator.

Gas 02 · serious signal

C2H2

Acetylene

Associated with arcing and very high temperatures, so even small amounts need attention.

Water · insulation ageing

H2O

Moisture in oil

Reduces dielectric strength and speeds up ageing of paper insulation.

Key line

An online two-gas monitor answers the question “is something developing, and how fast?”. Identifying the type of fault needs a full multi-gas analysis.

03Reading it

The rate matters more than the number.

Level
The concentration of a gas in the oil, in parts per million. A level means little without the transformer’s own history: design, age, loading and earlier samples all shift what is normal for a unit.
Rate of change
How fast the level is rising. A stable level, even a raised one, describes a past event or a stable condition. A rising level describes something active now. This is what an online monitor shows and a periodic sample cannot.
Hydrogen
The general signal. Because most fault types produce it, hydrogen is the broad alert. It says “look closer”, not “this is the cause”.
Acetylene
The serious signal. It forms only at very high energy. In a transformer where it has been absent, its appearance deserves prompt attention.
Moisture
Read with temperature. Water moves between paper and oil as temperature changes, so a moisture reading is read together with oil temperature and load. Its trend over days says more than one value.

04Can and cannot

What a two-gas monitor can and cannot tell you.

The honest version, before anything else. The monitor raises the question; the laboratory and the engineer answer it.

Terms used on this page

DGA
Dissolved gas analysis: measuring the gases dissolved in transformer oil.
ppm
Parts per million, the unit of gas concentration in oil.
Limit of detection
The smallest concentration the instrument can report.
Multi-gas / full DGA
Laboratory analysis of the full set of fault gases, used for fault-type interpretation.
Ratio methods
Interpretation methods that compare the proportions of several gases. They cannot be applied to two gases alone.

07After an alarm

The monitor raises the question. The laboratory answers it.

  1. 01 · Monitor

    Alarm.

    Hydrogen, acetylene or moisture passes a set limit, or rises faster than a set rate.

  2. 02 · Site

    Check the context.

    Load, oil temperature, recent switching or maintenance. Rule out an operational explanation.

  3. 03 · Sample

    Take an oil sample.

    Send it for full multi-gas laboratory DGA.

  4. 04 · Laboratory

    Interpret.

    Under IEC 60599 or IEEE C57.104. This is the step that points to a fault type.

  5. 05 · Engineer

    Cross-check and plan.

    Compare with partial discharge data and electrical tests. Decide on loading, further tests or an outage.

General sequence. Limits and procedures are set by the asset owner.

09Standards

Relevant standards

IEC 60599
Guide for interpreting full multi-gas DGA results from laboratory analysis.
IEEE C57.104
Guide for interpreting gases generated in oil-immersed transformers.

An alarm from an online monitor is normally followed by an oil sample interpreted under one of these guides. The guides apply to the laboratory result, not to the two-gas reading.

10FAQ

Questions we are asked

Q1Is InsuLogix G2 a full DGA monitor?

No. It measures two gases, hydrogen and acetylene, and moisture. It is an early-warning monitor. Fault-type diagnosis needs laboratory multi-gas DGA.

Q2Then why monitor only two gases?

Because they carry most of the early-warning information. Hydrogen appears in most developing faults. Acetylene points to high-energy faults. Together they say when to look closer.

Q3Can we stop taking laboratory samples?

No. The monitor fills the time between samples and tells you when to take one sooner. Routine laboratory analysis remains the basis for diagnosis.

Q4What does a moisture alarm mean?

That water content in the oil has risen. Moisture lowers the dielectric strength of the oil and speeds up paper ageing. Read it with oil temperature, because water moves between paper and oil as temperature changes.

Q5How does the monitor connect to our systems?

Per manufacturer data: Modbus RTU and Modbus TCP as standard, DNP3 and IEC 61850 as options.
