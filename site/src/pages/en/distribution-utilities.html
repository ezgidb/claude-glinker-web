Online Monitoring for Distribution Utilities

Where is the fault? Which asset is closest to failure? What should be maintained first?

A distribution company operates a large number of assets over a wide area and is measured on continuity and quality of supply. The practical questions are where a fault is, which assets are closest to failure, and where limited maintenance resources should go first. Each row below addresses one of those questions.

01The power chain

Few large assets. Very many small ones.

The chain widens as it goes. Each level asks for a different kind of monitoring.

  1. 01

    Primary substation

    Power transformers: few, large, slow to replace. Here depth of monitoring is justified.

  2. 02

    HV cable circuits

    Routes with joints and terminations spread along them.

  3. 03

    MV overhead feeders

    Long lines with branches. The task is locating the fault.

  4. 04

    Distribution transformers

    A large population of small units. One sensor per unit, not a system.

  5. 05

    Supply points

    Where the quality of supply is measured and reported.

  6. 06

    The whole fleet

    Where all of the above has to become one priority list.

At the top of the network, monitor in depth. At the bottom, monitor in breadth.

03Three questions

The three questions, one at a time.

Q1

Where is the fault?

On a long overhead feeder, finding the fault is often the larger part of the interruption. Line-mounted sensors and analytics software narrow the search to a section, and make visible faults that draw too little current to trip protection, such as broken conductors.

T3Cable & MV Monitoring {{> arrow}}

Q2

Which asset is closest to failure?

A primary transformer or an HV cable circuit gives little outward sign of a developing fault. Continuous measurement of partial discharge, dissolved gas and moisture, or joint temperature and screen current, shows a trend while the asset is in service.

Q3

What should be maintained first?

With thousands of distribution transformers and a fixed budget, the question is ranking. Per-unit oil-condition sensing, Class A power quality records and asset performance management software turn separate readings into one priority list.

04Questions

Questions this sector asks

Q1We cannot monitor everything. Where do we start?

Where failure costs most and replacement takes longest: usually the primary substation transformers. Then the feeders with the longest fault-location times.

Q2Does the feeder solution work on underground cable?

The line sensor described on this site is for overhead lines. For underground MV cable, tell us about the network and we will check the suitable route with the manufacturer.

Q3Can the analytics software run on our own servers?

Per manufacturer data, the grid analytics software can run on a manufacturer-hosted cloud server or on the customer’s own server. See Cable & MV Monitoring.

Q4Why does power quality measurement need to be Class A?

Because results that will be reported or discussed with a customer have to be comparable. Class A defines how each parameter is measured so that two instruments agree.

Q5We already have test data for years. Is it of use?

Yes. Asset performance management software uses offline test results and operating history as well as online data.
