01Why continuous

The asset looks the same on the day before it fails.

Most insulation and network faults do not appear suddenly. They develop over weeks or months, and for most of that time the asset keeps operating normally.

A periodic test records one moment; a fault that starts the week after goes unseen until the next test. A permanently installed monitor measures while the asset is in service, records the trend and raises an alarm when a set limit is passed.

Maintenance can then be planned around the condition of the asset instead of a fixed calendar or an unplanned outage.

  1. A — In service

    Measure in service.

    No outage is needed to take a reading. The asset is observed under its real load, temperature and voltage.

  2. B — Over time

    Record the trend.

    One value says little. The direction and the rate of change say whether to act.

  3. C — At a limit

    Alarm on a limit.

    A set limit turns a slow change into a clear moment for a decision, while there is still time to plan.

A single reading tells you where you are. A trend tells you where you are going.

What monitoring does not do

  • It does not repair anything. It gives time and information.
  • It does not replace offline testing. An alarm is usually followed by a targeted test or a laboratory sample to confirm the finding.
  • It does not see every failure mode. Each method watches specific phenomena, which is why the table below maps methods to assets.

Five words used on every page

Online
Measured while the asset is energised and in service.
Continuous
Measured permanently, not at intervals.
Trend
How a measured value changes over time.
Alarm limit
A set value which, when passed, triggers a notification.
Condition-based maintenance
Work planned from the measured state of the asset, not from the calendar.

06FAQ

Questions we are asked

Q1Does online monitoring replace periodic testing?

No. The two work together. Monitoring watches continuously for specific changes. Offline tests and laboratory analysis go deeper when a change needs to be confirmed or explained.

Q2Does the asset have to be taken out of service to be monitored?

Monitoring itself runs while the asset is in service; that is its purpose. Whether an outage is needed to install sensors depends on the asset and the sensor type, and is defined for each installation.

Q3Which assets should be monitored first?

Usually those where a failure costs most and a replacement takes longest. For most networks that starts with power transformers. The “what is different” column in the table is a good guide.

Q4Can existing sensors be used?

It depends on the system and the sensor. For partial discharge, see the ICMobserver section on the Partial Discharge Monitoring page.
